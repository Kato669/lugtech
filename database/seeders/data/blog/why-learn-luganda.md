Luganda is the language of the Baganda, the people of the old kingdom of Buganda in central Uganda. It is spoken every day in Kampala and across the central region by millions of people, as a first language or alongside English and Swahili. If you spend time in Kampala, you will hear it everywhere: in the market, on a boda boda, at the taxi park and around the family table.

So why learn it? Here are five good reasons.

## 1. Family

For many learners this is the biggest reason. Maybe you grew up in the diaspora and understand a little, but never speak. Maybe your grandparents speak Luganda and you want to talk with them, not just smile and nod. Even a simple [[Oli otya?]] ("How are you?") and [[Webale nnyo]] ("Thank you very much") changes how a visit feels.

## 2. Respect

In Buganda, how you greet people matters. Greeting an older man as [[Ssebo]] ("Sir") or an older woman as [[Nnyabo]] ("Madam") shows good manners. People notice when a visitor makes the effort, and they warm to you quickly.

## 3. Everyday life in Kampala

At the market, a few words go a long way. [[Ssente mmeka?]] means "How much?", and [[Kendeezaako]] politely asks the seller to lower the price a little. You will not need a dictionary to see the smile it gets.

## 4. Culture

Luganda carries a rich culture: clans and their totems, names that tell a family's story, and hundreds of proverbs (*engero*) that pack wisdom into a few words. You can explore them on the [Culture](/culture) pages of this app.

## 5. It's fun, and it is closer than you think

Luganda is written in the Latin alphabet, and its spelling follows the sounds closely: once you can read a word, you can say it. It also has patterns that click into place, like the way *omu-* (one person) becomes *aba-* (many people).

> Start small: a greeting today, a number tomorrow. A few minutes a day adds up quickly.
