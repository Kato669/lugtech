In Buganda, a greeting is not a quick "hi". It is a small conversation, and it matters. Taking time to greet someone properly shows respect, and skipping it can seem rude. Here are the greetings you will hear every day.

## How are you?

The everyday greeting is [[Oli otya?]] ("How are you?"). The usual reply is [[Bulungi]] ("Fine" or "Good"), or [[Gyendi]] ("I'm fine"). Friends of the same age might also say [[Ki kati?]] ("What's up?").

## Good morning

In the morning, people don't just say "good morning". They ask how you slept: [[Wasuze otya?]] The reply is [[Nsuze bulungi]], "I slept well".

## Good afternoon and good evening

Later in the day, the question becomes how your day has been: [[Osiibye otya?]] And you answer [[Nsiibye bulungi]], "My day has been good".

## Showing respect

When you greet someone older, add [[Ssebo]] ("Sir") for a man or [[Nnyabo]] ("Madam") for a woman. Traditionally, girls and women kneel when greeting elders, and boys may bow or kneel too. You will still see this in many homes today, especially in villages and at family gatherings. As a visitor, a warm greeting and a respectful tone are always welcome.

## Saying goodbye

When you leave, say [[Weeraba]] ("Goodbye"), or [[Tunaalabagana]] ("See you").

## Try it

Next time you meet a Luganda speaker in the morning, try this:

- You: [[Wasuze otya?]]
- They: [[Nsuze bulungi]]
- You: [[Kale]]
